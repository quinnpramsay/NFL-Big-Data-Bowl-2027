**# Plan**

1. Research
- Exploratory Data Analysis
  - Understand the available Combine and NFL tracking data, including sample sizes, missing values, and potential limitations.
- Outside research:
  - Look into different successful Big Data Bowl projects throughout the years and what made them stand out.
  - Look into players from those draft classes. Identify outliers, unexpected career trajectories, and possible ideas for reverse engineering.
  - Research existing football analytics to understand what has already been explored.
  - Theorize different hypotheses and discuss the best 5 routes of action.
- Testing
  - Test all 5 theories that we agree upon and see if we can identify 50 to 100 valid examples of that event happening within the player data.
  - Determine whether the Combine data has validity or measurable support for each idea.
  - Spend this time manually inspecting samples to make sure the events are being identified correctly and the metrics make sense from a football perspective.
  - Compare the feasibility of each idea and identify the project with the strongest measurable events and most potential for original findings.

2. Initial Development
- Define the main research question and the specific outcomes we want to measure.
- Develop the metrics that will be used to evaluate player movement.
- Establish a baseline using traditional Combine measurements.
- Build initial models and test whether the new movement metrics provide additional value.
- Account for differences in position, player role, opportunities, and playing time.

3. Back Testing
- Test the metrics against actual NFL performance.
- Compare results across different players and draft classes.
- Identify outliers and players whose NFL performance differs from what their Combine measurements would suggest.
- Test whether the findings hold up across different samples.
- Check for statistical significance and make sure the results are not driven by random correlations or a small number of players.

4. Visualizations
- Create visuals that clearly explain the movement metrics and what they measure.
- Show examples of individual players and specific plays where the metrics are useful.
- Compare players with similar Combine profiles but different NFL outcomes.
- Highlight the most important findings and where the metrics provide value beyond traditional testing.

5. Developing Report
- Explain the research question, methodology, and reasoning behind the metrics.
- Present the strongest findings with actual player examples and supporting statistics.
- Explain how the findings could be useful for NFL scouting, player evaluation, and development.
- Address limitations and areas where the data does not support a definitive conclusion.

6. Peer Review and Final Testing
- Have others review the project for football accuracy and statistical validity.
- Challenge the assumptions behind the metrics and look for weaknesses.
- Recheck the data, calculations, and results.
- Simplify anything that is difficult to understand or does not add value.
- Finalize the notebook, visualizations, and report for submission.
